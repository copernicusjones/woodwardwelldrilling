What a well costs here

Drillers quote the hole and the pump as separate things, and depth moves the total more than anything else you control.

Depth

Wells in northwest Oklahoma go deeper than people moving in from wetter country expect, because the shallow water is not dependable. You pay by the foot, and casing runs by the foot too.

Equipment in the hole

Casing, screen, and grout go in alongside the drilling. A hole that runs through sand and gravel gets cased differently than one that does not.

The pump

Pump, pressure tank, wiring, and the pitless adapter usually land on their own line. Figure a fifth to a third of the whole job.

From first call to water

A residential job here runs a few weeks start to finish. The drilling is the short part of it.

  1. Get on the schedule

    Ask for a rough date, not just a price. Slots move with the weather and with whatever the last job turned into.

  2. They come look at the ground

    Rig access, how far the hole sits from the septic and the property line, and where the tank and the line into the house will go.

  3. Drilling

    The rig sets up, the hole goes down, casing follows it. The driller logs the depth and what they hit on the way. One to three days for most houses.

  4. Pump, water test, paperwork

    The pump and pressure system get tied into the house, the water gets tested, and the driller files the completion report with the state. You end up with running water and a record of the well.

Questions people ask

How deep will my well be?

Nobody can tell you from a desk. It depends on where in the county you are, and drillers here often run several hundred feet before the water is dependable. Ask each one what depth they would budget for on your ground, and how they bill if they have to keep going.

Do I need a permit?

Oklahoma handles water wells at the state level, and the completion paperwork usually gets filed by the licensed driller as part of the job. Before work starts, ask your contractor what they file and whether you sign anything.

Will the water be safe to drink?

Test it. Groundwater around here can carry hardness, sulfur, iron, or nitrates depending on the spot, and you will not see any of that in a glass. A lab test costs a fraction of the well and tells you whether you need treatment before it reaches your plumbing.

Is a well cheaper than staying on rural water?

Usually over ten or twenty years, especially if the line is long or you are hauling. Compare the up-front well cost against the monthly bill plus tap and line fees. Run both numbers before you commit.

What if they don't hit water?

Ask before you sign. Drillers handle it differently, and the answer matters most in a dry year.

How long does a well last?

Casing done right lasts decades. The pump is what fails, somewhere between ten and twenty years. Treat a pump replacement as maintenance, not a disaster.
