What a well actually costs here

Most guides quote national numbers and scare people off. Woodward County has about 1,900 recorded wells and the state keeps the depth on nearly all of them, so the local picture is not a guess.

How deep

Half of the wells in this county are between 60 and 155 feet. A quarter of them finish under 100 feet. Only about one in eight goes past 250. That is shallow by national standards, and it is the single biggest reason a well here costs less than people budget for.

Where the water sits

Median static water level is 40 feet down. Median tested yield is 30 gallons a minute, and a house needs five to ten. Water is not usually the problem in this county. Getting a good hole and a properly sized pump is.

One thing to get in writing

Have the quote break out the hole from the pump. A single bundled number from one driller cannot be compared against a bundled number from another, and that is where people overpay.

How the job goes

For most houses here it is a few weeks end to end. The rig is only on your land for a day or two of that.

  1. Get on the schedule

    Ask for a date, not just a price. In a wet spring or a dry summer the wait moves a lot, and a cheap quote three months out does not help if you need water now.

  2. They walk the ground

    Where the rig can get in, how far the hole sits from the septic and the property line, and where the pressure tank and the line into the house will land.

  3. Drilling

    Most Woodward County holes finish in a day. Casing goes in behind the bit and the driller logs the depth and what they hit on the way down.

  4. Pump, test, paperwork

    The pump and pressure system get tied in, the water gets tested, and the driller files the completion report with the state. You end up with running water and a permanent record of the well that stays with the property.

Questions people ask

How deep will my well be?

In this county, probably not deep. Half of the recorded wells land between 60 and 155 feet and the median is 98. Ask each driller what depth they would budget for on your ground and how they bill if they have to keep going past it.

Do I need a permit?

Oklahoma handles water wells at the state level through the Water Resources Board. Ordinary household and livestock wells are generally exempt from the permit requirement, while larger commercial and irrigation work is not. The completion report gets filed by the driller either way. Ask your contractor what they file and whether you sign anything.

Will the water be safe to drink?

Test it. Groundwater here can carry hardness, sulfur, iron, or nitrates depending on the spot, and none of that shows up in a glass. A lab test costs a fraction of the well and tells you whether you need treatment before it reaches your plumbing.

Is a well cheaper than staying on rural water?

At $5,400 to $9,000 for a complete system, usually yes over ten or twenty years, especially if the line is long or you are hauling. Compare it against the monthly bill plus tap and line fees and run both numbers before you commit.

What if they don't hit water?

Ask before you sign. Drillers handle a dry hole differently, and the answer matters most in a dry year. Given how shallow the wells run here it is uncommon, but uncommon is not never.

How long does a well last?

Casing done right lasts decades. The pump is what fails, somewhere between ten and twenty years. Treat a pump replacement as maintenance, not a disaster.
